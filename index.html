Critical Infrastructure Update

What Happened to annas-archive.org? Is Anna's Archive Closed?

Clearing up confusion: Why the original .org domain stopped responding and where the archive is operating today.

If you recently tried opening https://annas-archive.org/ or annas-archive.li, you likely received a DNS_PROBE_FINISHED_NXDOMAIN, Server Not Found, or serverHold error. Many users incorrectly assumed the entire project was shut down permanently.

The reality: Anna's Archive was not shut down. In early 2026, the Public Interest Registry (PIR)—the US-regulated organization managing the .org top-level domain—placed the domain on serverHold following aggressive copyright lawsuits initiated by corporate publishers and library consortiums (including the OCLC lawsuit). Shortly thereafter, European ccTLDs like .li faced similar registry suspensions.

🛡️ The Archive Resists: The Move to New TLDs and Decentralized Mirrors

Because Anna’s Archive is completely open source and distributed across decentralized BitTorrent and IPFS nodes, the files and catalog can never be deleted by seizing a single domain name. The team immediately migrated operations to resilient domains:

  • annas-archive.gl: Primary operational domain.
  • annas-archive.se: Secondary European mirror.
  • annas-archive.is & annas-archive.pk: Active regional mirrors.
  • Tor Onion Hidden Services: Censorship-resistant onion addresses immune to domain registrars.
Universal Knowledge Preservation

What is Anna’s Archive?

A comprehensive, non-profit metadata aggregator and shadow library dedicated to cataloging and preserving all of humanity's recorded literature and scientific publications.

Founded in late 2022 following law enforcement actions against digital repositories, Anna’s Archive arose as an emergency preservation project. Named in honorary tribute to pioneering open-access activist Aaron Swartz and library archival initiatives, the project was created by a community of volunteer archivists called "Anna".

Unlike traditional websites that store files on a single vulnerable central server, Anna’s Archive acts as a meta-search engine and distributed archival hub. It scrapes, verifies, deduplicates, and organizes metadata from every major existing repository—including Library Genesis (LibGen), Sci-Hub, Z-Library, DuXiu, and the Internet Archive.

"The mission of Anna’s Archive is to ensure humanity’s collective knowledge is never lost to server confiscations, digital decay, censorship, or commercial paywalls. We believe in universal, frictionless access to education for every human on Earth."

Engineering & Infrastructure

Inside the Anna’s Archive Technical Architecture

How does Anna's Archive manage to index over 105 million publications without collapsing under petabytes of storage costs or centralized domain seizures? The system relies on four core architectural pillars:

1. Automated Metadata Ingestion & Deduplication Algorithm

Anna's Archive runs automated scrapers and synchronization daemons that ingest database dumps from Library Genesis, Sci-Hub, Z-Library, DuXiu (China’s massive academic digital scanning project), and Internet Archive collections.

Because the same book often exists under different filenames, scanned resolutions, or ISBN variants across LibGen and Z-Library, Anna's Archive utilizes a proprietary MD5 and metadata reconciliation engine. It parses author names, titles, publisher data, and file hashes to unify duplicates under a single canonical record while preserving multiple alternative download mirrors.

2. Decentralized Peer-to-Peer Backups (IPFS & BitTorrent)

Unlike centralized corporate repositories, Anna's Archive does not rely solely on direct-attached web servers. All files are bundled into standardized torrent packages (typically 100GB to 500GB chunks) and seeded by independent volunteers globally.

Furthermore, each record is mapped to its IPFS CID (Content Identifier). This allows users with IPFS nodes to retrieve documents directly from the InterPlanetary File System without relying on any centralized HTTP server or DNS registry.

3. Open Source Stack & Open Data Dumps

The software stack powering Anna's Archive is developed publicly. Anyone can audit the backend code, run a local mirror, or contribute optimizations. The entire database of metadata—including bibliographic records, OCR text extracts, and mirror links—is distributed via weekly public torrent dumps, ensuring that no single legal action or server seizure can ever destroy the index.

Troubleshooting & Fixes

Common Access Issues & How to Solve Them

Encountering download errors, slow speeds, or blocked pages? Here is how to resolve them immediately.

🚨 "Website Blocked / DNS Probe Finished"

Cause: Your local ISP has blacklisted old domain names (like .org or .li) via DNS spoofing.
Immediate Solution: Use our mirror selector to switch to annas-archive.gl or annas-archive.se, and configure your device DNS to Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google DNS (8.8.8.8).

⏳ "Slow Download Queue / Wait Time"

Cause: Free partner servers enforce a short delay (usually 15-30 seconds) to prevent automated bot scrapers from consuming server bandwidth.
Immediate Solution: Allow the countdown timer to finish. Once the timer reaches zero, the direct download URL triggers automatically.

📉 "Download Speed is Slow"

Cause: High traffic on a specific mirror host.
Immediate Solution: Click on "External Downloads" on the mirror page to choose alternative routes (such as LibGen mirrors, Sci-Hub IPFS, or BitTorrent magnet links). A download manager like Free Download Manager also accelerates multi-part downloads.

🔒 "Security Warning / SSL Error"

Cause: Visiting expired phishing clones or untrusted copycat domains.
Immediate Solution: Always verify you are connected to authentic official mirrors (annas-archive.gl, annas-archive.se). Official domains run valid Let's Encrypt / Cloudflare SSL certificates.

Key Definitions

Glossary of Shadow Library & Archival Terms

Demystifying the terminology of modern digital archival and decentralized repositories.

  • Shadow Library: An online repository providing free access to digital collections of literature and research papers otherwise locked behind commercial paywalls.
  • DOI (Digital Object Identifier): A unique, persistent alphanumeric string assigned by the International DOI Foundation to scholarly documents and journal papers.
  • ISBN (International Standard Book Number): A 10- or 13-digit universal identifier assigned to published physical and electronic books.
  • MD5 Checksum: A 128-bit cryptographic hash value represented as a 32-digit hexadecimal number used to verify identical binary files.
  • IPFS (InterPlanetary File System): A peer-to-peer distributed hypermedia protocol designed to store and access files based on content hashes.
  • LibGen (Library Genesis): A pioneer shadow library founded in 2008 providing free searchable access to millions of academic textbooks and novels.
  • Sci-Hub: An open science project founded in 2011 providing direct, free access to over 88 million academic journal articles.
